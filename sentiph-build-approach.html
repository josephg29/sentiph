Your question

Reverse-engineer Claude's workflows, or just copy them?

Honest answer: neither, exactly — and the reason is a small but important distinction hiding inside the question. Once you see it, the best path is obvious.

The short answer

Copy the pattern — it's free and public. Don't reverse-engineer the engine — it's proprietary, brittle, and you don't need it. Then build one thin “conductor” that's unique to Sentiph, riding on Claude's own official tools for the hard parts.

Why it's a false choice

Two different things are tangled together

Your question quietly mixes up two things that deserve different answers:

The pattern

The assembly-line idea

Fan the work out → build → check → fix → gather. Nobody owns this. It's a public, well-understood way of organising work. Copying it is free, smart, and the obvious move.

The engine

Anthropic's private machinery

The exact code that runs their version. It's proprietary, it changes whenever they ship, and you'd be guessing at it. Reverse-engineering this is the risky, pointless path.

So the framing isn't “reverse-engineer vs copy.” It's “copy the free idea, skip the locked box, and build only the small part that's truly yours.”

The three real options

Laid out side by side

AVOID

Reverse-engineer their engine

🚫

Try to figure out Anthropic's private machinery and rebuild it. Don't. It breaks every time they update, it's a legal grey area, and it's effort spent cracking a safe to steal a recipe that's already printed on the box. There's nothing in there worth the risk.

WON'T FIT

Clone their whole system into Sentiph

🧩

Bolt a copy of their entire workflow system onto Sentiph. Two problems. One: the shapes don't match — their workflows use throwaway helpers, while Sentiph runs lasting terminals on a canvas; it won't just snap in. Two: you'd be chained to maintaining a copy of someone else's moving target. A lot of work to end up brittle and behind.

BEST

Adopt the pattern · ride official tools · thin conductor

✅

Take the free public pattern. Let each Sentiph agent use Claude's own built-in tools for the actual work — Anthropic maintains and improves those, so you inherit upgrades for free. Then build the one small piece that's uniquely Sentiph: moving work between terminals, the pass/fail checkpoint, and the final gather-up. Minimum to build, maximum to keep.

The one-line way to remember it

Copy the recipe, not the restaurant.

The assembly-line recipe is public — take it. The appliances already exist and someone else services them — use those (Claude's official tools). The only thing you actually build is how orders flow through your food truck's stations. You don't break into the restaurant, and you don't try to fit their whole kitchen into your truck.

What to actually do

The plan, in four moves

  1. 1

    Take the pattern as-is

    No reverse-engineering. The build → check → fix → gather idea is public; just use it.

  2. 2

    Let each agent ride Claude's official tools

    Sentiph's children are already full Claude agents. Let them use Claude's own orchestration for the work inside a task — so when Anthropic improves it, Sentiph gets better for free, with zero effort from you.

  3. 3

    Build one thin conductor — the Sentiph-only part

    The bit nobody else can give you: handing work between terminals on the canvas, the pass/fail checkpoint, and rolling everything into one clean result. Small, focused, and genuinely yours.

  4. 4

    Ship one recipe, prove it, then expand

    Get a single “normal coding job” line working end to end before adding the smart picker or the careful line. Don't boil the ocean.

Why this is the best option

It wins on every axis that matters

💸 Cheapest to build

You reuse what exists and build only a thin layer — not a whole engine.

⏳ Most durable

Claude's tools improve under you. A reverse-engineered copy would only rot.

⚖️ Legally clean

A public pattern plus official tools — no prying into anyone's internals.

🐙 Stays true to Sentiph

The canvas-and-terminals identity stays yours; you only add the conductor.

⚠️ The one mismatch to keep in mind

Claude's dynamic workflows lean on throwaway helpers that spawn, do one job, and vanish. Sentiph runs lasting terminals you watch on a canvas. That's why pure copying can't simply “drop in,” and exactly why the thin conductor has to be yours — it's the translator that makes the public pattern speak Sentiph's language. Good news: that translator is small. It's the only real thing you're building.

Bottom line

Don't reverse-engineer a problem that's already solved in public. Copy the idea, stand on Claude's official tools, and build only the thin layer that makes the pattern fit Sentiph.
